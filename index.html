Plants chosen for Central Texas

A lot of yards fail because they're planted with things that were never going to survive here. We plant what thrives in our heat, our soil, and our rainfall.

  • Dwarf yaupon hollyinstead of boxwood

    Boxwood scorches in August. Yaupon is a Texas native that stays tidy and green through the heat.

  • Turk's capinstead of hydrangea

    Hydrangeas want acidic soil we don't have. Turk's cap blooms red all summer and the hummingbirds find it.

  • Gulf muhlyfor fall color

    Clouds of pink plumes every autumn, tough enough to ignore the rest of the year.

Native plantings use less water once established, and replacing thirsty turf may qualify for Austin Water rebates. We'll point out where that applies to your property.
